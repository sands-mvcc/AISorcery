Participation Requirements

What Every Participant Must Submit

As part of the participation requirements for the AI Cohort, each participant is required to submit a brief project description along with a corresponding Statement of Work (SOW) outlining the activities, deliverables, and expected outcomes of the project.

📝

Project Description

A concise narrative (1–2 pages) identifying the discipline, the AI technology or tool being applied, the target student audience, and the educational problem being solved.

📋

Statement of Work (SOW)

A structured document specifying activities, timeline milestones, measurable deliverables, and expected student and institutional outcomes. Template provided at orientation.

🧑‍💻

Classroom Implementation

The developed tool or resource must be piloted in at least one live course section during the grant period and results shared with the cohort community.

🔬

Evaluation and Reflection

Participants collect student feedback and self-assess alignment with project outcomes. A brief reflection report is submitted to the external evaluator at semester close.

🗂️

Repository Contribution

All deliverables — code, lesson plans, assessment rubrics, and case studies — are uploaded to the shared GitHub repository under Creative Commons license for national dissemination.

🤝

Cohort Participation

Participants attend the summer in-person workshop plus monthly online sessions and present project results at the CyAD Conference or a designated regional showcase.

Eligible Focus Areas

Project Focus Areas

Projects may address any of the following areas related to artificial intelligence in the community college environment. Projects that support effective, ethical, and sustainable AI use are especially encouraged.

🎓

AI in Teaching & Learning

Integration of AI tools into instructional practice — including personalized learning, AI-powered tutoring, automated feedback, simulation-based learning, and curriculum design support.

⚙️

AI Adoption Challenges & Opportunities

Research and practical projects addressing barriers to adoption, faculty preparedness, student AI literacy, equity and access, academic integrity, and institutional change management.

🏛️

Policy, Governance & Strategy

Research, analysis, and documentation related to institutional AI policies, data governance frameworks, privacy compliance, ethical guidelines, and sustainable implementation roadmaps.

Template

Statement of Work (SOW) Components

Every participant SOW must address all components below. Use the provided template, available at orientation and on the cohort portal. Only use the sections that are relavent to your project.

Suggested SOW Sections

Project Title
A descriptive name for the AI-integrated project specific to your discipline.
Discipline / Course
The program area and specific course(s) where the project will be deployed.
Problem Statement
The instructional challenge, gap, or opportunity the project addresses. Include relevant workforce or industry context where applicable.
AI Tool(s) Used
The AI technologies, platforms, or APIs to be integrated (e.g., generative AI APIs, machine learning frameworks, NLP tools, computer vision). Include justification for selection.
Activities
Step-by-step plan: research, design, development, pilot, evaluation, and dissemination. Each activity should have a responsible party and target completion date.
Deliverables
Tangible outputs: prototype tool, lesson plan, lab exercise, video demo, case study, assessment rubric. At least one deliverable must be GitHub-publishable.
Expected Outcomes
Measurable student learning outcomes and faculty development outcomes. Specify how success will be assessed and documented.
Ethical Considerations
How the project addresses data privacy, algorithmic bias, academic integrity, and equitable student access to AI tools.
Timeline
Month-by-month milestones from project kickoff through final repository submission and conference presentation.
Sustainability Plan
How the project will be maintained and updated after the grant period, including plans for integration into the regular course curriculum.
How It Works

Participation Process

Projects move through five structured phases during the cohort year, with support from the project team, external evaluator, and national network at every step.

1

Orient & Plan (Summer Workshop — Day 1)

Attend the in-person summer workshop. Receive the SOW template and project examples. Work with cohort peers and facilitators to identify your discipline's AI opportunity and draft your project concept.

2

Design & Develop (Summer Workshop — Day 2 + Fall Semester)

Complete and submit your SOW within 30 days of the workshop. Begin tool or resource development, participating in monthly online mentoring sessions for guidance and peer review.

3

Pilot in the Classroom (Spring Semester)

Deploy your AI-integrated resource in at least one live course section. Collect student feedback data and document outcomes. Refine based on real classroom evidence.

4

Evaluate & Report (End of Spring)

Submit your reflection report and outcome data to the external evaluator. Participate in the cohort debrief session. Finalize all repository materials for publication.

5

Disseminate & Celebrate (CyAD Conference)

Present your project at the Cybersecurity Across Disciplines (CyAD) Conference or a designated regional showcase. All published materials go live in the national AI content library.

Example Projects

12 Model Participant Projects

The following examples illustrate the scope, depth, and variety of projects suitable for AI Cohort participation. Filter by discipline area, then expand any card for a sample SOW summary.

🎤
Speech / Communication
VirtualAudience — AI-Powered Speech Practice Environment

Students deliver live speeches to a simulated AI audience that responds with facial expressions, body language cues, and real-time engagement signals, then generates a structured performance debrief.

Key Deliverables
  • Browser-based virtual audience simulator with customizable crowd size and demographics
  • AI feedback engine scoring delivery pace, eye contact, vocal variety, and filler words
  • Student self-assessment rubric aligned to course competencies
  • Instructor dashboard showing aggregate class performance trends
Expected Outcomes
  • Increased student willingness to practice outside class hours
  • Measurable reduction in speech anxiety through repeated low-stakes rehearsal
  • Improved rubric scores on formal graded speeches
AI Tool: Generative AI + Computer Vision
Problem Statement

Students avoid speech practice due to self-consciousness; limited class time means only one or two graded speeches per semester. Low rehearsal frequency correlates with high anxiety and poor performance.

AI Tools

OpenAI API (feedback generation), MediaPipe (posture & eye-contact detection), Web Speech API (transcript & pace analysis).

Ethical Considerations

Video data processed locally; no recordings stored. Students opt-in. Bias audit of feedback language performed prior to pilot. Accommodations available for students with disabilities affecting delivery norms.

🔧
Automotive Technology
AutoDoc AI — Intelligent Repair Documentation & Estimating Tool

Students use natural language and photo input to generate professional repair orders, parts lists, labor hour calculations, and customer-ready service documentation — mirroring real shop workflows.

Key Deliverables
  • AI-driven repair order generator accepting text descriptions and under-hood photos
  • Labor time lookup integrated with industry flat-rate databases
  • Parts cost estimator pulling live API pricing data
  • Printable customer estimate and technician job sheet templates
Expected Outcomes
  • Students produce industry-standard documentation at the end of the first semester
  • Reduced errors in labor time calculations on practical assessments
  • Faculty document a 30% reduction in time spent on documentation instruction
AI Tool: Vision API + LLM
Problem Statement

Automotive students struggle with professional documentation standards — a core employability skill. Manual instruction is time-intensive and students rarely see real-world volume or variety of repair scenarios.

AI Tools

GPT-4o Vision (photo-based diagnostic assist), OpenAI API (repair narrative generation), NAPA or Mitchell1 API integration for parts pricing, custom HTML/JS front-end.

Ethical Considerations

Students learn to verify AI-generated labor estimates against authoritative flat-rate manuals. Project explicitly addresses AI output validation as a workforce competency.

History / Social Sciences
ArtifactIQ — AI Historical Artifact Analysis & Source Critique Tool

Students submit images or descriptions of historical artifacts and receive structured AI analysis across four critical dimensions — historical bias, factual accuracy, source consistency, and cultural context — then compare AI analysis to scholarly sources.

Key Deliverables
  • Artifact analysis interface accepting image upload or text description
  • Structured report template covering bias, accuracy, consistency, and provenance
  • Side-by-side comparison view: AI analysis vs. peer-reviewed source
  • Critical thinking reflection worksheet for student self-assessment
Expected Outcomes
  • Students demonstrate source critique skills using disciplinary vocabulary
  • Increased engagement with primary source analysis in asynchronous courses
  • Students identify and articulate at least two AI limitations per artifact
AI Tool: Vision API + LLM Analysis
Problem Statement

History students rarely engage with primary sources deeply enough to evaluate bias and consistency. Traditional assignments lack immediate feedback, limiting the iterative thinking historical analysis demands.

AI Tools

GPT-4o with vision (artifact description and contextual analysis), custom prompt chain enforcing bias/accuracy/consistency/provenance framework, HTML5 single-file app hosted on GitHub Pages.

Ethical Considerations

Project foregrounds AI limitations on historical accuracy as a core learning objective. Students must critically evaluate AI output using scholarly sources — AI serves as a thinking partner, not an authority.

🛡️
Cybersecurity
SOCSimAI — Adaptive Security Operations Center Simulator

An AI-driven SOC simulation where students receive a realistic stream of alerts, logs, and threat intelligence. An AI analyst persona guides triage decisions, escalation paths, and incident response documentation in real time.

Key Deliverables
  • Simulated SIEM alert dashboard with configurable scenario difficulty
  • AI analyst NPC that responds to student triage decisions with branching feedback
  • Incident response report template auto-populated from student decisions
  • 10 scenario packs aligned to CompTIA Security+ and CySA+ objectives
Expected Outcomes
  • Students complete incident response workflows without instructor intervention
  • Demonstrated alignment with NIST IR framework in student-generated reports
  • Measurable improvement on SOC-related lab performance assessments
AI Tool: LLM + Scenario Engine
Problem Statement

SOC analyst roles require pattern recognition and decision-making under pressure — skills that cannot be developed from textbooks alone. Commercial simulation platforms are cost-prohibitive for most community colleges.

AI Tools

Claude API or GPT-4 (adaptive scenario narration and triage feedback), static JSON scenario packs (no external threat data needed), HTML5/JS single-file app deployable on GitHub Pages or MVCC VTLC.

Ethical Considerations

No real threat intelligence or student-identifiable data used. Scenarios use fictional organizations. Students learn responsible disclosure and chain-of-custody documentation as part of the simulation workflow.

Mathematics
MathViz AI — Interactive Mathematical Operation Visualizer

Students type or speak a math concept — from basic algebra to calculus — and receive an animated, step-by-step visual simulation paired with an AI tutor that explains the operation in plain language at an adjustable reading level.

Key Deliverables
  • Concept visualization engine covering arithmetic, algebra, geometry, statistics, and calculus topics
  • AI tutor responding to follow-up questions in plain English
  • Reading-level toggle (middle school / college / technical) for differentiated instruction
  • Practice problem generator with step-check feedback at each stage
Expected Outcomes
  • Improved scores on conceptual understanding items in pre/post assessments
  • Increased out-of-class self-study engagement tracked via usage analytics
  • Reduced rates of repeated math course enrollment (developmental math context)
AI Tool: LLM + D3.js Visualization
Problem Statement

Developmental and gateway math students struggle to connect procedural steps to conceptual meaning. Static textbook diagrams and one-size-fits-all explanations leave visual learners and non-traditional students behind.

AI Tools

OpenAI API (explanation generation at variable reading levels), D3.js or Chart.js (dynamic math visualizations), MathJax (rendered equations), Web Speech API (optional voice input).

Ethical Considerations

Tool designed to scaffold understanding, not replace problem-solving practice. Instructors configure whether solution steps are shown or withheld to prevent shortcut use on graded work.

Chemistry
LabSafeAI — Virtual Chemistry Lab Safety & Reaction Simulator

Students conduct simulated wet-lab experiments using an AI lab partner that enforces safety protocols, predicts reaction outcomes, explains molecular behavior, and flags hazardous procedure errors before the student "proceeds."

Key Deliverables
  • Virtual lab bench interface for common gen-chemistry experiments
  • AI safety officer persona that intercepts unsafe student actions with explanations
  • Reaction outcome predictor covering acid-base, redox, and synthesis reactions
  • Pre-lab and post-lab report generator aligned to course learning objectives
Expected Outcomes
  • Students pass lab safety assessment at higher rates prior to physical lab access
  • Increased confidence entering the physical lab as measured by pre/post survey
  • Online and hybrid students achieve equivalent lab understanding to in-person cohorts
AI Tool: LLM + Simulation Engine
Problem Statement

Online and hybrid chemistry students lack access to hands-on lab experience. Safety comprehension gaps create liability concerns when students arrive for the first physical lab session with insufficient preparation.

AI Tools

Claude API (safety feedback and reaction explanation), PubChem API (molecular data lookup), custom HTML5 lab bench UI, optional WebGL molecular visualization layer.

Ethical Considerations

Clearly labeled as simulation, not a substitute for physical lab requirements where mandated. AI safety feedback reviewed by department chair and safety officer prior to release. FERPA-compliant — no student data stored.

Nursing / Healthcare
PatientSim AI — Clinical Scenario & Patient Interaction Trainer

Nursing students engage in text-based clinical conversations with AI patient personas presenting realistic symptoms, histories, and emotional states. The system scores assessment technique, therapeutic communication, and documentation accuracy.

Key Deliverables
  • AI patient persona library covering 20+ common clinical presentations
  • SBAR documentation generator populated from student interaction transcript
  • Communication technique scorer (empathy, clarity, active listening)
  • Instructor scenario builder for custom patient cases
Expected Outcomes
  • Improved NCLEX-PN communication and documentation scores
  • Students complete more clinical scenario practice hours outside scheduled sim lab
  • Faculty report higher readiness in student cohorts entering clinical rotations
AI Tool: LLM Persona + Scoring Rubric
Problem Statement

Clinical simulation lab time is scarce and expensive to staff. Students need repeated, low-stakes patient communication practice before high-stakes clinical rotations, but current options require instructor presence.

AI Tools

GPT-4 with system-prompt persona definition (patient simulation), custom scoring rubric mapped to therapeutic communication competencies, HTML5 chat interface, session transcript export for portfolio evidence.

Ethical Considerations

Patient personas are entirely fictional. No real patient data used. Limitations of AI simulation clearly communicated to students. Faculty validate all clinical content before deployment to ensure accuracy.

Business / Accounting
FinDecisionAI — Business Case Analysis & Decision Support Trainer

Students input a business scenario (startup funding, inventory decision, hiring plan) and an AI advisor generates a structured cost-benefit analysis, financial projections, and a devil's advocate counterargument — then students defend their own recommendation.

Key Deliverables
  • Business scenario intake form with AI-driven financial analysis output
  • Side-by-side view: AI recommendation vs. student recommendation comparison
  • Automated counter-argument generator to stress-test student reasoning
  • Written defense rubric and peer-review protocol for class discussion
Expected Outcomes
  • Students produce higher-quality written business recommendations on final projects
  • Improved critical thinking scores on business case exam items
  • Students articulate AI as a decision-support tool, not a decision-maker
AI Tool: LLM + Financial Modeling
Problem Statement

Business students often treat case analysis as a formulaic exercise rather than genuine decision-making practice. The lack of immediate feedback on reasoning quality limits development of the analytical skills employers prioritize.

AI Tools

OpenAI API (scenario analysis and counterargument generation), custom HTML5 case analysis interface, optional spreadsheet export for financial modeling, peer collaboration features using shared session links.

Ethical Considerations

Students explicitly taught that AI financial projections are probabilistic estimates. Rubric includes a required section where students critique the AI's analysis — building healthy skepticism as a professional competency.

English / Writing
DraftCoach AI — Iterative Writing Feedback & Revision Partner

Students submit essay drafts and receive targeted, rubric-aligned feedback in three revision passes — global (thesis and structure), paragraph (coherence and support), and sentence (style and mechanics) — with each pass unlocking the next only after revision.

Key Deliverables
  • Three-pass feedback engine with instructor-customizable rubric mapping
  • Revision tracking view showing change between drafts
  • Source integration checker flagging quotation, paraphrase, and citation issues
  • Faculty portal with aggregate class draft-quality trends over the semester
Expected Outcomes
  • Average final draft quality scores improve compared to prior semesters
  • Students submit more revision cycles per essay than in control sections
  • Reduced plagiarism incidents through source-integration coaching during drafting
AI Tool: LLM + Rubric Engine
Problem Statement

Faculty cannot provide detailed feedback on multiple drafts for all students in large sections. Students who receive feedback only on final drafts have no opportunity to apply learning within the same assignment cycle.

AI Tools

Claude API (feedback generation with instructor-defined rubric as system context), diff-viewer for revision comparison (custom JS), optional integration with existing LMS assignment submission via iframe.

Ethical Considerations

Tool provides feedback on student writing — it does not write for students. Institutional AI policy acknowledgment required before first use. Faculty retain final grading authority; AI feedback is advisory only.

HVAC / Manufacturing Technology
DiagnostIQ — AI Fault Diagnosis & Predictive Maintenance Trainer

Students input sensor readings, error codes, and symptom descriptions from HVAC or manufacturing equipment scenarios. The AI diagnoses the fault, ranks repair priority, and generates a preventive maintenance schedule aligned to industry standards.

Key Deliverables
  • Fault diagnosis interface accepting sensor data and error code input
  • AI-ranked repair priority list with cost-impact justification
  • Predictive maintenance schedule generator aligned to OEM specifications
  • 15 scenario packs covering HVAC, PLC, and conveyor system faults
Expected Outcomes
  • Students develop systematic fault-isolation methodology before shop access
  • Improved scores on diagnostic reasoning components of program assessments
  • Faculty document increased student confidence in equipment troubleshooting
AI Tool: LLM + Sensor Data Modeling
Problem Statement

Diagnostic reasoning is the highest-value and hardest-to-teach skill in technical programs. Equipment access is limited, and real faults cannot be induced safely in a classroom setting.

AI Tools

OpenAI API (fault reasoning and maintenance scheduling), custom scenario JSON library built by faculty from real service records (anonymized), single-file HTML5 app with no backend required.

Ethical Considerations

Students explicitly taught to verify AI diagnostic conclusions against manufacturer service manuals before any real-world action. Tool clearly labeled as training simulation — not a certified diagnostic instrument.

Criminal Justice
EthicsEngine — AI Bias & Accountability in Criminal Justice Analyzer

Students examine real and simulated criminal justice AI systems (risk assessment tools, predictive policing, facial recognition) through structured scenario analysis, evaluating accuracy, bias, due process implications, and policy alternatives.

Key Deliverables
  • Case study database of 12 documented AI-in-criminal-justice deployments
  • Bias analysis framework tool with structured evaluation categories
  • Policy brief generator scaffolding student arguments with evidence prompts
  • Mock hearing simulation where students present and defend policy positions
Expected Outcomes
  • Students articulate constitutional and ethical implications of AI in criminal justice
  • Improved analytical writing quality on policy-focused course assignments
  • Students demonstrate understanding of algorithmic accountability frameworks
AI Tool: LLM + Case Analysis Framework
Problem Statement

AI decision-support tools are rapidly entering criminal justice practice, yet most CJ curricula have not addressed AI ethics, algorithmic bias, or technology accountability — leaving graduates unprepared for these emerging professional challenges.

AI Tools

GPT-4 (scenario analysis and policy brief scaffolding), curated public dataset integration (ProPublica COMPAS data, ACLU documentation), structured Socratic questioning prompts for guided analysis.

Ethical Considerations

All case studies use public record data only. Multiple perspectives — law enforcement, defendant, civil liberties, community — are required in every analysis. Tool is explicitly critical of AI deployment, not promotional.

Information Technology / Administration
PolicyForge AI — Institutional AI Policy Builder for Community Colleges

Faculty and administrators use a guided AI-assisted wizard to draft, review, and refine institutional AI use policies — covering academic integrity, data governance, accessibility, and procurement — customized to their college's size, mission, and accreditation context.

Key Deliverables
  • Guided policy wizard with branching questions based on institution type and context
  • AI-drafted policy sections with editable placeholders and rationale notes
  • Comparison view against AACC, EDUCAUSE, and state-system policy examples
  • Policy gap analysis report identifying missing governance components
Expected Outcomes
  • Participating institutions produce a first-draft AI policy within the grant year
  • Faculty report increased confidence in institutional AI governance knowledge
  • Tool adopted by at least five NCyTE network institutions beyond the pilot cohort
AI Tool: LLM + Policy Framework
Problem Statement

Most community colleges lack formal AI governance policies, yet faculty, students, and staff are already using AI tools daily. Policy gaps create inconsistent enforcement, equity concerns, and accreditation risk.

AI Tools

Claude API (policy drafting with institutional context from wizard responses), curated policy corpus (EDUCAUSE, ACE, state system examples), HTML5 wizard interface with section-by-section draft export to Word/PDF.

Ethical Considerations

Tool generates policy drafts only — final policy requires human governance review and board approval. Wizard explicitly surfaces equity, accessibility, and due-process considerations in every policy section.
