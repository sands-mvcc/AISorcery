Project Summary

Proposal of the Project

The technical and career workforce must not only comprehend AI technology but also identify innovative ways to leverage it to enhance job performance and add value to their organizations. Building on the success of the Cybersecurity Across Disciplines (CyAD) Conference, Moraine Valley Community College (MVCC) proposes a three-year, comprehensive project to meet labor market demand for workers with artificial intelligence aptitude.

Unlike projects focused on AI curriculum within computer science alone, this NSF ATE program is specifically designed to prepare graduates of two-year institutions pursuing career and technical jobs across healthcare, automotive technologies, business integration, automation and manufacturing, HVAC technologies, and criminal justice. The project will collaborate with faculty nationwide to develop content, workforce competencies, and instructional materials hosted in a shared, open-source repository.

🎯

Intellectual Merit

Addresses a critical gap: the practical application of AI within the technical and career workforce trained by community colleges, enhancing operational efficiency, decision-making, and cybersecurity resilience.

🌐

Broader Impacts

Recruits faculty from underrepresented communities, partners with the NCyTE National Faculty Development Academy, and disseminates results nationally through conferences and open repositories.

Roadmap

Goals and Objectives

Goal One — Produce Interactive Instructional Materials for Teaching AI in STEM Programs

  • Develop ten modular, interactive instructional materials covering machine learning, neural networks, data analysis, and AI ethics
  • Design hands-on labs and projects applying AI to real-world scenarios
  • Integrate cutting-edge tools: AI agents, topic-specific chatbots, deep learning/neural networks, image recognition
  • Pilot and evaluate materials across 15 community college STEM programs

Goal Two — Establish a National AI Teachers and Learning Academy

  • Build a comprehensive faculty training program in AI technologies, teaching methods, and curriculum design
  • Offer five hybrid training workshops annually serving 100 faculty per year
  • Host AI workshops and sessions at the CyAD Conference
  • Develop micro-credentials in partnership with leading AI organizations

Goal Three — Establish a National AI Community of Practice

  • Launch an online collaborative platform for sharing resources and teaching initiatives
  • Host local faculty development events and recurring virtual meetings
  • Curate a shared repository of instructional materials, case studies, and research
  • Promote interdisciplinary AI research and showcase events

Goal Four — Target and Work with All Communities

  • Create outreach initiatives raising AI career awareness in all communities
  • Partner with local organizations and schools to deliver workshops and informational sessions
  • Provide access to AI tools to address the growing AI digital divide
  • Build mentorship programs connecting students with AI professionals and educators

Why This Project

Rationale for the Project

As an NSF ATE National Center for Cybersecurity for the past three years, the NCyTE Center has worked closely with MVCC and a national network of business and industry partners — including Microsoft Corporation, Cisco Systems, Meraki, ESPO Systems, Dell SecureWorks, and Advocate Health Care Systems. This collaboration identified a growing need to incorporate AI-generated data and processes into business operations, particularly in operational technologies and cybersecurity.

In response, NCyTE established the Cybersecurity Across Disciplines (CyAD) Conference, now operating for two years, which integrates cybersecurity and AI knowledge across business, health sciences, manufacturing, automation, IT, and automotive technology programs. Attendee feedback consistently underscored the necessity of integrating AI education across diverse disciplines — not just computer science.

The technical and career workforce requires competencies distinct from those pursuing AI development roles: the ability to apply AI concepts practically, validate AI output, and recognize the limits of systems that are "only as intelligent as the data and knowledge used to program them." This project responds directly to that workforce demand by bringing together faculty from computer science, IT, manufacturing, business, and health sciences to build and disseminate real-world AI instructional content.

Program Details

The project will establish communities of practice by hosting summer workshops at twelve community colleges in different regions of the country, leveraging existing NCyTE partnerships:

1. Whatcom Community College
2. Las Positas College
3. St Petersburg Community College
4. Moraine Valley Community College
5. Brookdale Community College
6. Anne Arundel Community College
7. Sinclair Community College
8. Fayetteville Technical College
9. El Paso Community College
10. Honolulu Community College
11. Miami Dade Community College
12. Pikes Peak State College

Five workshops of 20–25 participants will run each summer: two days of in-person instruction followed by monthly online continuing education and mentoring sessions.

Participant Stipends

6-8 faculty participants per institution will be eligible for a participation stipend paid by MVCC annually. The stipend is based on participation and deliverable. The stipend will be $1,500 – $2,500 based on level of participant and SOW.

Instructional Materials & Supplies

Each participant receives AI advanced access and the opportunity to attend regional and national AI related events.

Professional Growth

Participants gain access to AI micro-credentials developed with leading AI organizations, recognition at the national CyAD Conference, and continued mentoring through monthly or quarterly online sessions.

Institutional Benefits

Access to creative commons open-source AI content repository, and opportunities for ongoing collaboration with NSF ATE centers including NITIC, CORD, CREAT, FLATE, NCAT, and GeoTec.

📝 Cohort Application 📄 Download W9 Form

Faculty accepted into the National AI Teachers and Learning Academy commit to the following:

  • Attend a two-day, in-person summer workshop plus monthly online continuing-education and mentoring sessions
  • Develop a classroom-ready example of AI use, such as a chatbot, lesson plan, assessment tool, data analytics activity, or individualized student study plan
  • Create an activity that enables students to apply AI in a practical workforce scenario relevant to their discipline
  • Submit developed materials for review, editing, and validation before inclusion in the open-source online library
  • Share knowledge, experience, and developed content with colleagues at their home institution and/or region
  • Participate in project evaluation activities, including surveys, focus groups, and interviews
  • Engage with the National Community of Practice platform and the annual CyAD Conference

All instructional materials, lesson plans, labs, case studies, and faculty-developed activities produced through the project will be reviewed, edited, validated, and published in a centralized, open-source online library.

Hosting & Access

Content is hosted in a collaborative, version-controlled repository on GitHub, including source code and revision history so materials can be properly tracked, reviewed, approved, and updated. The library is freely accessible with no cost to use, and is publicly posted on the EPNC and NCyTE websites.

Licensing

All developed content is shared and disseminated under a Creative Commons license, tailored to the needs of community college technical and career programs.

Library Foundation

The NCyTE Center has already produced a library of over 20 examples to inspire ideas and innovation among workshop participants, forming the foundation this project will expand.

Sustainability

Participants are encouraged to continue creating new and innovative AI applications and contribute them to the repository, ensuring the library grows and remains current well beyond the grant's three-year funding period.
